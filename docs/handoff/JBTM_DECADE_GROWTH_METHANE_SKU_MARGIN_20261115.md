# JBTM 10-year growth, methane-regulated protein substitution, product SKU expansion and operating margins
**As-of 2026-11-15 KST.** Scope: Stock PM JBTM primary; PAHC remains second core only. Status: `RESEARCH_SCENARIO / NO_BUY_APPROVAL`. Hypotheses not verified forecasts. This note corrects the earlier misunderstanding of the **1.3% number**.

## Executive answers
1. **1.3%** was an **assumed conservative 2026–2035 forward JBTM revenue CAGR** in a simulation; **NOT actual 2026 growth**. Actual JBTM 2026 Jan–Sep consolidated +4.3% reported sales, **+1.8% organic** (FX +2.5pp); latest Q3 2026 reported −0.7% YoY. 2028 company organic CAGR goal 5–7% vs 2025.
2. **10-year historical JBT FoodTech sales**: $634.7M in 2014 → **$1,716.0M 2024 continuing food technology**. Same broad food-business lineage, before 2025 Marel acquisition but not perfectly invariant perimeter. **2014–2024 10-period reported CAGR = 10.4574%**, arithmetic mean of 10 annual YoY changes **10.995%**. Does **not equal organic CAGR**: many earlier JBT business acquisitions and 2018 ASC606 revenue recognition boosted FY2018 reported FoodTech revenue by ~$114M; AeroTech was disposed and old FoodTech segment then current continuing perimeter are similar, but not certified identical accounting. 2025 JBT+Marel 2024→2025 consolidated reported **+121.3%** is a discrete acquisition, so it must **not** be included in a growth trend estimate.
3. The short 2026 organic pace is below historical reported growth but **not proven to be the worst organically in 10 years**, because a consistent historical 10-year organic series has not been reconstructed; 2019−2.4%, 2020−7.1% reported FoodTech and 2023 +4.6%, 2024 +3.1% continuing comparable shows a range.
4. **Methane**: ruminant beef/dairy has large enteric methane; pigs far lower; neither category's global consumption is proven to decline through 2035. OECD–FAO 2026 baseline projects global poultry **+20%**, beef **+8%**, pig meat **+4%**, total food aquatic demand **+12%**, farmed aquaculture production **+18%** relative 2023–25 base. Regional decline per person in high income markets vs global volume expansion distinguish. Denmark 2030 effective agricultural GHG tax is one real policy example, **not US/worldwide supply restriction**. If rules become material, cost/price transmission and product substitution depend on subsidies, mitigation, leakage and preferences.
5. **JBTM benefits potentially from substitution**, but is also a seller of other-meat processing equipment. 2025 equipment order mix poultry **44%**, seafood **6%**, meat (beef/pig etc) **16%**, other 34%. These order ratios are **not sales ratios**. As a first-order **volume-only** model the company-wide uplift is small; the **more relevant growth engine** is processed-product SKU proliferation, yield/portioning/packaging automation, equipment CAPEX intensity, integration sales, software/service.
6. **2028 company margin target 20% is adjusted EBITDA, NOT GAAP operating margin**. Actual TTM GAAP operating margin ending 2026-09-30 ~**7.3%**, Q3 standalone **10.1%**; 2026 guided adjusted EBITDA margin **17.25–17.50%**, 2028 **20%** vs 2025 **15.8%**. PM illustrative FY2028 **GAAP operating margins 9%, 11%, 13%** (do not mistake for guidance; normalized amortization and merger cost are critical).
7. **Real technology & internal optimization exists**: customer side ATHENA high yield/adaptive poultry de-boning, machine vision IRIS quality, integrated lines, portioning fish/further processed products, remote service. Company internal side VAVE standardized control panel **40–50% cost decrease for the CONTROL PANEL alone**, supplier consolidation, standardized procurement, site footprint improvement, continuous improvement systems. Marel savings **2025 realized $43M, exit-run-rate $85M, 2027-end projected run-rate $150M**. Need recurring investment returns and market share evidence to treat gains as durable after 2028.

## Methane regulation plus protein substitution: mechanism, risks, bounded sensitivity
- Climate evidence: Our World in Data / Poore-Nemecek global food kgCO2e/kg item averages Beef(beef herd) **99.48**, pork **12.31**, poultry **9.87**, average farmed fish **13.63**, farmed prawns **26.87**. These are LCA **all GHG** averages (including CO2/N2O) not methane-only, fish type/customer tech dependent; NOT direct same kg of edible protein. Cattle ruminants strong enteric methane, pigs not same. Global poultry not automatically lower-impact than all fish, and fish consumption not substitutable gram-for-gram with live-weight figures.
- Denmark OECD 2026 describes 2030 agricultural livestock tax **DKK120 per tCO2e effective** after 60% allowance, 2035 DKK300, with feed additives and subsidies; region-specific, animal species differ. 2026 OECD-FAO baseline already includes its own policy assumptions; below are **incremental shocks relative to 2035 baseline**, not forecasts that global absolute beef/pork consumption declines.
- Hypothetical incremental 2035 **demand shocks to JBT equipment customer markets** (all values by 2035 vs OECD base, not annual):
  | Market | Mild incremental scenario | High incremental scenario |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | Poultry | +3% | +8% |
  | Seafood | +2% | +5% |
  | JBT's **meat** end market (combined beef/pork equipment demand) | −2% | −5% |
  For the *fixed 2025 equipment order weights*, incremental equipment order market volume signal `0.44×Poultry + 0.06×Seafood + 0.16×Meat` = **+1.12%** (mild) or **+3.02%** (high) cumulatively. Assuming *by fiat* equipment shipments track those market volume changes 1:1, and the ordered equipment categories scale to ~**50% of company revenue**, **company overall cumulative 2035 sales lift** = +0.56% / +1.51%, over 9 years ≈**+0.062pp/yr / +0.167pp/yr** respectively, other sales fixed. **These are sensitivity illustrations**, not backtests. Real CAPEX capital intensity, JBT market share and product mix can amplify or reverse. Cannot model all meat sales as actually declining because pigs behave differently from beef. "Meat" 16% share not separately identified, so −2/−5% is an intentionally restrictive composite assumption.
- Global 2035 beef/pork **physical consumption** under OECD baseline still grows +8%/+4% over base; climate pressure could reduce incremental growth or increase local substitutions without absolute declines.
- Direct result surprisingly small for company-wide growth: because JBT sells meat equipment as well, chicken/fish combined ~50% of equipment orders, half all revenue broadly recurring, and shifts are *incremental* to existing baseline. The **greater option** is accelerated facility investment and SKU-driven automation, which could allow end-market equipment CAPEX grow faster than animal tonnage. This is a separate hypothesis that needs actual orders by customer, not a guaranteed regulatory windfall.

## SKU proliferation and automation thesis: what exists
- Company Investor Day 2026 states consumers demand boneless ready-to-eat/prepared foods and variety, "SKU expansion"; U.S. National Chicken Council long-term marketing shares have shifted away from whole birds to cutups/processed, although percentages are not standardized here.
- JBT Marel equipment supply extends poultry basic slaughter/cut-up to high-margin adaptive breast de-boning, per-size automated portioning, machine-vision quality inspection, ready-meals forming, chilling/freezing, line control, packaging, and after-sales support. Each additional process may increase equipment spend and maintenance/service revenue **per kilogram output** and customer switching cost. Frozen ready meals/labor and complex packaging contribute in Prepared F&B, not simply Protein.
- Official ATHENA system capacity up to **6,000 products/hour** and individual adaptive software, with one company-advertised **>60% labor reduction for specific loading/harvesting/trimming steps** in a customer case; not universal factory labor reductions. Poland's Cedrob / Plukon verified company case, but economic ROI not externally audited.
- Fish whitefish portioning equipment and filleting, packing exist; actual 2025 fish **6% of equipment orders**, fish profit significantly below mature poultry (~5–10% adjusted EBITDA margin quoted 2026 Q3 business vs 22.4% whole Protein division). Don't extrapolate all seafood demand (shrimp, bivalves, low-cost freshwater species) as Marel equipment orders.
- Company cross-sale order case study: Indonesia further-processing, Poland nuggets, Brazil portioning, US secondary poultry. 2025 order synergies ~$30M (as company reported), **not incremental annual earnings**.
- Disconfirming conditions: consumers seek cheap whole poultry, small plants manual/low-cost equipment, local OEM wins, multi-SKU changeovers lower utilization, processing production outsourced, no proprietary data access/strong pricing power, fish economics dilute segment margin, other meat hardware loses demand. No deterministic margin expansion from product complexity.

## Actual internal redesign & cost savings
- FY2025 savings realized $43M, December 2025 exit run-rate ~$85M; projected December 2027 run rate ~$150M. Company expected FY2026 **additional in-year realized savings ~$60M**, FY2027 ~$30–40M, FY2028 ~$10–15M. These are acquisition synergies with a finite ramp, not recurring annual incremental improvements forever.
- Product modularity / VAVE project: standardized modular **control panel** architecture across product lines reported **40–50% CONTROL PANEL cost reduction** and engineering hours/time; **not machine/factory cost**, no audited company-scale impact quantified. Production and supplier consolidation, lean Kaizen/RCI daily management, logistics, system integration and other efficiency projects ongoing. Operational effectiveness in 2026 Q3 helped margins but 2026 Q2 PFB production/logistics caused delays. Maintain both evidence and counterevidence.
- FY2028 20% **adjusted EBITDA margin** is moderately plausible, not a reported GAAP EBIT target; Obin BofA Sell headline cited aggressive targets/execution risk. A cautious PM model: **2028 GAAP EBIT margin 10–11% in baseline**, much above 2026 TTM GAAP ~7.3% but close to recent Q3 annualization ~10.1%; other scenarios 9% and 13%, contingent on adjustments falling as deal costs fade.
