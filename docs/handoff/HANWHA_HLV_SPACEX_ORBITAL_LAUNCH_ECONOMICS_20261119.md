## 3. Hanwha economic bridge (avoid confusing revenue with equity value)
- **Engineering:** production and launch integration from Nuri help, but methane propulsion and a new integrated orbital vehicle require their own qualification, reliability and flight history.
- **Commercial model:** external satellite launches, sovereign-security demand and contracted launch services are possible revenue streams. Internal Hanwha Systems launches **must be evaluated under consolidation elimination at the relevant accounting perimeter**, not simply added to group sales.
- **Illustrative scenario only:** KRW **10 billion revenue per launch** × 5 / 10 / 30 external annual launches = KRW **50 / 100 / 300 billion** launch-service revenue; no assertion of HLV pricing, capacity, demand, profitability or schedule.
- **Valuation:** revenue is not earnings. Need annual launch count, contract revenue per launch, payload kg, variable manufacturing/propellant/test/insurance cost, spaceport fixed expense, R&D and CAPEX amortization, failures, launch utilization, interest and minority-interest impacts. Compute **incremental owner FCF and ROIC** vs capital cost.
- **Gate states:** design → engine ground qualification → flight attempt → orbital insertion → multiple reliable missions → third-party contracted backlog → launch-level contribution margin → positive after-CAPEX cash flow. Do not promote 2028 target or 2029 commercial aspiration to achieved success.
- **Comparable moats:** SpaceX benefits from flight cadence, reusable hardware and Starlink captive constellation operations. Hanwha may enjoy Korea's sovereign-launch preference but the available data do not show comparable global cost/kg.
- **Risk:** 23T KRW spending plan could impair shareholder returns if the revenue ramp and project ROIC disappoint. Defense backlog/cash conversion remains a separate driver of Hanwha earnings.

## 4. SpaceX investment thesis and falsification
- **Moat thesis:** low marginal cost, high launch cadence, capacity reservation and mission assurance may make SpaceX a preferred transport provider if multi-ton orbital compute infrastructure scales.
- **Countercase:** orbital data centers may be technologically possible but less economic than terrestrial solutions; launch demand may remain specialized; governments can fund rival national launch systems; SpaceX may face price pressure and high Starship R&D/CAPEX.
- **Non-orbital base:** existing launch services and Starlink business make upside **not binary**, but their cash generation, valuation and capital needs must be independently underwritten.
- **Access caution:** verify SpaceX's latest securities/listing status and accessible share class before ever treating it as a buyable stock; do not assume an account can purchase it.
- **Monitoring:** Starship payload-to-orbit, reflight cost and cadence, reusable development status, external-launch gross margin, Starlink cash conversion, orbital-DC signed cargo demand, competitor launch price/capacity and government reserved demand.
