# JBTM vs PAHC — Same-date comparison, inventory, governance, product breadth
**As of:** 2026-11-15 KST; 2026-11-13 US closing share prices. **Status:** CORE_2_JBTM_PAHC / NO_BUY_APPROVAL. **PM judgment:** JBTM business/FCF preferred on present evidence, not a buy authorization or proven higher total return.

## 1. Owner's open questions
1. Which is better? JBTM: verified installed automation platform for poultry, meat, fish; commercial AI/AXIN; 2026 operating cash conversion. PAHC: animal health consumables with broad species reach, potentially less tied to a customer factory CAPEX cycle, lower earnings multiple. **JBTM more compelling to research/own at present**, conditional on valuation and true recurring organic growth.
2. 90.3% PAHC voting control: BFI holds 19,496,034 Class B shares carrying **10 votes/share**, plus 56,152 Class A; about 48% economic stake vs **90.3% total votes** (2026-08-21). Risks: small shareholder's inability to replace directors or challenge M&A/capital allocation, conflicts and reduced outside takeover premium. Benefits possible: committed long-term ownership. **NOT automatic share-price fall or numerical governance discount**; test related-party transactions, ROIC, dividends and acquisitions.
3. Does strong sales fix cash flow? It can, if collected cash and inventory liquidation exceed fresh working-capital needs and capex. Growth may itself soak up cash through stocked goods/receivables. **Company-documented inventory builds do not prove unsold/unmarketable inventory**.
4. Is PAHC an animal-protein producer with every medicine for all species? No: **supplier of veterinary drugs, anticoccidials, feed additives, special nutrition, vaccines, minerals**, rather than direct meat/fish producer. Offers ~345 Animal Health product lines, ~790 overall company product lines in 90 countries. Target animals poultry, swine, beef/dairy cattle, aquaculture and some companion animals. Does NOT sell every drug, treat every disease, or automatically apply chicken medicines to fish. Fish sales not separately reported.

## 4. Products and relative protein transition flexibility
- PAHC FY2026 sales ($M): MFAs and other **810.728 (53.4%)**, Mineral Nutrition **282.341 (18.6%)**, Nutritional Specialties **195.072 (12.9%)**, Vaccines **156.419 (10.3%)**, industrial Performance Products **73.533 (4.8%)**. “MFA” contains antibacterial and anticoccidial products plus some non-drug/ethanol processing aids: **not all is antibiotic**.
- PAHC FDA/Brazil antimicrobial regulation risk is concrete (Brazil virginiamycin FY2026 sales ~$27M; U.S. Mecadox ~$21M and withdrawal *proposed*, not final). Non-antibiotic vaccines/health supplements may compensate, but there is no disclosed proof they can fully offset exposure. Some PAHC specialty nutrition serves **dairy cows** (OmniGen, Animate): not fully immune to cattle methane pressure. Aquaculture business is real but **fish sales/profit undisclosed**.
- JBTM 2026 nine months ~51% “recurring” revenue from **parts, services, equipment rebuilds/leases, subscriptions**; **not pure AI software recurring**. Protein Solutions $1.396B/total $2.911B (48%); Prepared Food and Beverage $1.515B (52%): not a pure protein business. Seafood was ~7% of equipment orders in the earlier period; no proof market shift automatically boosts JBTM margins.
- JBTM risk: customer's capital spending cycles, machine design/installation cost, competitors, Marel acquisition integration, organic growth only +1.8%, 2026 Q3 revenue -0.7%, $1.694B gross debt. PAHC risk: regulatory approvals and animal-specific uses, commodity mineral business, concentrated family control, inventory and cash turnover.

## 5. Underwriting judgment and reversal gates
**Present preference: JBTM** because higher demonstrated absolute and relative cash conversion, installed machinery/service base and specific AI products, ability to serve multiple protein processing markets. Despite higher earnings/EV-EBITDA multiples, **JBTM TTM measured equity FCF yield ~5.1% vs PAHC ~1.5%**. Do NOT claim JBTM is guaranteed cheaper or will have superior forward stock returns. Its GAAP FY2026 forecast P/E ~28x, adjusted ~15x: disclose adjustments.
**PAHC could become better** if (a) inventory contracts over several quarters without markdowns, (b) normalized shareholder FCF rises toward earnings, (c) vaccines and nutritional specialties truly replace regulated antibacterial growth promoters, (d) measured fish/aquaculture revenue and animal-specific IP show profitable growth, and (e) controlling family allocates cash to debt reduction and above-cost-of-capital returns. At current ~$1.46B cap, 5% recurring equity FCF yield implies ~$73M annually, far above recent $21.67M TTM actual. JBTM has to prove organic expansion/AI pricing power and integration delivers durable margins.

- **Supplemental periods, not same quarter:** JBTM first 9 months 2026 revenue +4.3% (organic +1.8% / FX +2.5pp), operating margin **214/2911=7.35%** (prior 117/2790=4.19%). - **JBTM protein segment** 2026 Q3 revenue +6.3% and segment adjusted EBITDA margin 22.4%; it is **not JBTM consolidated GAAP operating margin**.
- **Moat verdict:** JBTM **plausibly stronger in integrated equipment switching costs, process engineering/software, installed base and replacement parts/service relationship**, but stronger *quantified* company-wide moat is **not independently proven**. 2026 Q3 recurring revenue: Protein $239M and Prepared/Food/Beverage $263M = $502M / $994M ≈50.5% — **spares/aftermarket/services/equipment leases AND subscriptions, not 50% AI SaaS**. PAHC has country-/product-specific regulatory registrations, commercial formulations, distribution and recurrent consumables, but generic substitution, antimicrobial rules and product commodity competition. Regulatory barriers can create **strong moats in some PAHC lines**; no blanket ranking product-by-product.
- If the Owner's preference is durable engineering switching costs and broad food-protein adaptation, JBTM is the **current preferred investment research candidate**; high margin PAHC is not evidence of lower moat. JBTM's +1.8% organic 9-month growth and −0.7% Q3 revenue still fail to establish moat pricing power or high growth. No purchase recommendation.
