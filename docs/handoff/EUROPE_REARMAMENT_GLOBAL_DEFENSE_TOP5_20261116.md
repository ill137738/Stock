## Main thesis, primary proof
- [NATO Hague summit commitment 2025, updated June 2026] By 2035 allies pledge **5% of GDP** composed **>=3.5% core defense** and **up to 1.5% security/resilience/infrastructure**; 2026 core EU+Canada about 2.5% and EU NATO average 2025 2.3%. Rising budgets are signed *national investment targets*, not purchase orders, and not all spent on procurement or paid to suppliers. Joint defense cooperation between allies increases even as tensions rise; "end of human collaboration" too categorical. https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/defence-expenditures-and-natos-5-commitment
- EU SAFE instrument **€150B loans**, in force May 29 2025, **2026-10-29 Commission 2nd round €21.3B** invitation, 19 state agreements approved/16 signed, ~€13.5B disbursed to 11 member states; loan mechanism NOT EU direct €150B firm purchase commitments. Priority missiles, air/missile defense, ammunition, counter-UAS, military electronics. https://www.consilium.europa.eu/policies/safe/ ; https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/commission-invites-member-states-use-eur213-billion-under-safe-boost-defence-readiness-and-support-2026-10-29_en
- Critical **SAFE Article 16 procurement eligibility**: for common procurement with SAFE funds the **component cost from outside EU/EEA EFTA/Ukraine cannot exceed 35%** of the product's component cost, plus EU/EEA/UA establishment/control and special exception terms. Applies to program-specific SAFE-financed purchases, **not a blanket embargo on Korean equipment in all European markets**; Hanwha needs local content/economic presence, transfer and JV to maximize access, already sells through bilateral deals. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/1106/oj
- Risk: government fiscal constraints, debt interest rates, national industrial policy/procurement cycles, fixed-price defense manufacturing cost inflation, depleted advanced missile supply and quality-control, capital required for expansions, customer-advance-related FCF bumps and later reversals, political risk and normalized P/FCF multiples. Military conflict/closed trade can harm defense suppliers' own input availability and lower broad equities/ valuations, not automatically benefit stocks.
