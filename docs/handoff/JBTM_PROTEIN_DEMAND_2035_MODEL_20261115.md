## 2. Simple bottom-up volume-transmission calculation
An **illustrative physical demand baseline** for the equipment-order categories, with the actual JBT market revenue/volume elasticity **unknown**, is:
`0.44×(1.20^(1/10)-1) + 0.06×(1.12^(1/10)-1) ≈ 0.008779565 = 0.878% / year` of *total equipment orders*, **assuming** orders in each category scale 1:1 with the matching end-market physical consumption growth and all other equipment categories are flat. Use all aquatic food +12% rather than aquaculture +18%, since JBTM's reported 6% includes wild as well as farmed species.
- If one also **assumes** equipment/non-recurring project revenue is about 50% of consolidated revenue, and that 2025 order mix scales to revenue mix (not verified), consolidated annual volume-demand-derived contribution is `0.5×0.878%=0.439 percentage points`. This is a **partial-channel illustration**, not JBTM's documented projected growth nor a statistical lower/upper bound. Installed equipment aftermarket services also benefit later, not captured directly.
- Aquaculture-only 18% growth is not substitutable for *all aquatic demand 12%*. Using 18% for 6% total seafood order share would overstate if equipment captures nonfarmed fish. Their 2025 fish portfolio could be skewed toward whitefish, salmon etc vs mollusc/crustacean growth.
- Underlying physical food demand grows ~1–2% p.a., while automated **plant CAPEX** can grow 3–4% because penetration, process complexity, yield improvement, automation modernization and price/mix evolve. Underlying farm and processing volumes need not translate into one-for-one equipment purchase; shares, product mix and installed revenue lag matter.

## 3. Quantified sensitivity to 2035 (NOT analyst/company projections)
**Model start**: FY2026 company revenue guidance midpoint **$3.975 billion**; project end FY2035 9 calendar years after 2026. **All numbers nominal USD, constant FX, no further M&A**. Build three annual *organic* revenue scenarios by transparently combining *disjointly intended but empirically unproven* drivers:

`G = OtherSegmentAndRecurringOrganicGrowth_pp + 0.50 × [0.44×PoultryVolumeCAGR + 0.06×AquaticFoodDemandCAGR] × EquipmentCapitalIntensityElasticity + SeparateUpsellShareGains_pp`

The 0.50 is a **very simplified equipment revenue fraction** inferred from 50% "nonrecurring" company revenue, not official disclosed distribution of equipment shipments by type. Avoid treating the calculated result as true observed sales elasticity. Effects are intended to be distinct: volume/capital-intensity of JBT poultry/fish equipment, growth from unaffected other businesses/service, non-overlapping incremental share gains/integrated cross-sales and software monetization. In practice they may interact/double count; calibrate to actual results before investment.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 0.75 | 0.329 | 1.0 | 0.0 | **1.33%** | **$4.477B** |

**Arithmetic**: `FY2035 Revenue = 3.975 × (1+G)^9`. Values from mathematically exact unrounded CAGR, rounded for display. All parameters other than OECD volume CAGR, the investor equipment mix and 2026 sales starting point are **PM scenario assumptions**, not externally statistically estimated inputs. Far right is a scenario **not** a forecast of revenue probability or share price. No prediction of market share/time series uncertainty should be treated as confidence interval.

**Crosscheck alternative**: Company target 5–7% growth applies **2025–2028 only** and includes projected revenue synergies; it cannot be assumed to continue for nine years through 2035. If the 2026 base had 5/6/7% annual growth for 2027–2028 then moderated to 3/4/5% through 2029–2035, FY2035 revenue becomes **$5.39/$5.88/$6.40B**. This is illustrative, not guaranteed and not equivalent to the company's 2025–28 CAGR. 2035 base area **$5.6–5.9B** under these separate approaches.

## 4. How poultry/fish market transition could exceed volume growth
1. **Greenfield investment and formalization**: Asian, Latin American and African commercial poultry processing capacity and export-grade cold chain require more industrial plants than just production growth; some new capacity supplied by low-cost/local equipment competition. Use orders/capacity/proposals by region as observable signals, not general OECD tonnage.
2. **Upgrade of existing lines**: Upgrade from raw/whole birds to higher-margin cutup/deboning/portioning, breaded/ready meals and traceability; **equipment value per kg** could rise even if total meat kilograms do not. Protein Solutions and downstream Prepared Food overlap, but food/beverage 55% is a potential dilution of protein-specific tailwind.
3. **Labour/yield incentives**: ROI on +0.5pp carcass yield, lower waste and throughput may outweigh wage savings even in low-wage economies; evidence from controlled customer examples, not unverified performance averages.
4. **Recurring services and digital**: installed machines create spare parts, software subscriptions, maintenance and rebuilds. 50% broadly recurring sales does *not* mean software ARR or unique data rights.
5. **Fish-specific economics**: total aquaculture +18% over ten years but parts like molluscs/shrimps/low-priced freshwater fish require **different machines, price points, processing formats**; JBT Marel fish portfolio may be strongest in salmon and whitefish (only small subset). JBT fish segment margin ~5–10% currently vs Protein Solutions aggregate 22.4%, so faster fish sales could dilute short-term margin.
6. **Competition/regulatory risk**: BAADER/Meyn and local equipment OEMs, plant financing, biosecurity (avian influenza), import policies and trade, electricity/water/cold chain capabilities may interfere. No definitive >7% long-run organic trajectory from OECD alone.

## 5. Working judgement and verification gates
- **Industry volume demand supports multi-year resilience and incremental growth, but not a 10% organic company CAGR on its own.**
- **Near-term 2025–2028 company organic target 5–7% is plausible but not yet proven**: 2026 9mo reported organic +1.8%, Q3 group −0.7%; 3–4% company market growth requires market CAPEX > food tonnage growth, ~1% cross-sell and 1–2% projects require real execution.
- **My long-term base planning 4% organic sales CAGR** (not a probability-weighted statistical forecast), conservative 1–3%, strong execution 5–7%. The simpler explicit scenario model yields 1.3/4.0/5.8%, which is sensitive to made-up capex elasticity and program gains.
- **Higher shareholder return need not require >7% revenue growth**, as margins, FCF conversion, debt and repurchases can drive EPS over time. But cost synergy has a finite runway; post-2028 EPS CAGR must be grounded in lasting growth or capital allocation. No share-price CAGR projected.
