## 1. Owner's scenario and investment question
- Trade cooperation could weaken as wars, strategic rivalry and supply-chain protectionism intensify. Sovereign redundancy and re-shoring raise input, energy, labor and financing costs.
- If inflation persists and real/nominal interest rates remain at or above neutral for longer, which industries gain structural demand rather than simply benefiting from price inflation?
- Which innovations raise output per labor-hour, per watt and per unit of capital enough to offset cost-push inflation—possibly creating **sectoral disinflation/relative-price declines**, rather than necessarily broad CPI deflation?
- Seek companies with **defensible value capture**, recurring cash conversion, high incremental ROIC and valuations that do not prepay all projected growth.
- This is a **scenario, not a claim that world trade has ended or high rates are guaranteed**. State policy and monetary regime contingencies.

## 2. Dual-lens thesis
**A. Sovereignty/economic security spend:** defense and missile defense, power grid/transformers, reliable generation/storage, semiconductor fabrication and supply security, satellite launch/secure communications, cybersecurity.

**B. Cost-reduction/productivity:** industrial automation/robotics, industrial AI, semiconductors with better work-per-watt, design/manufacturing software, logistics optimization, food processing automation and grid-efficiency controls.

**Intersection:** localization/redundant supply chains create *higher baseline costs* and greater incentive to automate factories and improve power-use efficiency. However, customer CAPEX budgets, tariffs, borrowing costs, local servicing and industrial cycles can postpone orders.

## 4. Durable economics and selection framework
- Industry output growth ≠ vendor revenue ≠ diluted EPS/FCF ≠ shareholder return.
- **Productivity gain** does not necessarily imply CPI deflation: margin retention, bottlenecks and demand response may absorb the savings. The investable question is whether suppliers **capture a durable share** of customers' savings without competitors arbitraging it away.
- A country can simultaneously increase strategic expenditure (inflationary duplicated facilities) while firms invest in process innovation (disinflationary unit costs).
- Higher nominal policy rates are not automatic from geopolitics alone. Test different inflation/rate/capital-market conditions, not a single irreversible forecast.
- Stronger candidate traits: customer ROI, service/installed base, recurring or mission-critical revenue, moderate leverage, sustainable FCF after stock compensation and capex, dilution control, incremental ROIC, realistic entry valuation.
- Weak: long-duration pre-revenue narratives, debt-intensive capacity additions without firm contracts, passive raw-material inflation exposure mistaken for moat, growth already embedded in expensive multiples.
