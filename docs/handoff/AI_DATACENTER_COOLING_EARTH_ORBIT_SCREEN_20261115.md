## Space technology: reality and engineering gates
- **NASA State-of-the-Art of Small Spacecraft Technology, Thermal Control (May 2026)**: spacecraft external radiation through radiators, space thermal control using **heat pipes, deployable radiators, pumped liquid loops, phase change/storage, specialized surfaces**. Q-Rad labeled Redwire, but smallsat use.
- **GAO Apr 2026 Spotlight GAO-26-109012**: large orbital AI centers and cooling scale **unproven**. 5,000 square-foot (~465 m²) radiator conceivable for small satellite cluster module; high area/mass; solar array/power, communications, radiation, refit, and collision issues. For on-orbit liquid loops, internal conduction/convection can transfer heat but only **radiation** rejects heat externally in vacuum.
- **BCG Aug 27 2026**, current deployable lightweight radiators estimated tech maturity **TRL6**, two-phase liquid loops **TRL5**, full readiness **5–10 years**; *illustrative* 100 kW computer requires about **400m² radiator** (depends coolant/radiator temperature, solar illumination, emissivity, orientation, both sides). Estimates orbital computing costs still **~1.5× terrestrial in next decade** in realistic scenario. Orbital shares 10–15% of global AI market by 2040 are BCG's speculative modeling, **not bankable**.
- **Google Project Suncatcher** first prototype launched **Oct 1 2026** and returned first TPU machine-learning response (hello world) reported **Oct 30 2026**; radiation/thermal-stress tests still in progress. Google heat pipes and radiators in thermal-vacuum tests before orbital launch; 2027 planned satellites. One TPU hello-world != commercial compute facility or utility cost parity. Stock GOOGL is highly diluted exposure to this technological route, not a thermal supplier play.
- Orbital radiators are not merely "same liquid cooling gear sent to space": need radiation hardening, material compatibility, microgravity two-phase stability, foldable mass-efficient radiators, micrometeoroid tolerance, high emissivity/low solar absorption, coolant survivability and long service life. Today no listed company independently verified supplying both high-volume terrestrial AI cooling and validated **orbital AI data-center thermal radiators at commercial megawatt scale**. Search for contracting evidence before claiming dual-play.

## Terrestrial technology
- **NVIDIA June 21 2026** Vera Rubin design: **100% closed-loop liquid cooling at server level**, inlet coolant **45°C**, ~55°C exit in example; warm liquid can allow outdoor **dry coolers** instead of mechanical chillers in suitable climates. This structurally favors cold plates, CDUs, manifolds, monitoring, dry cooling, integrated leak/flow systems. It may **reduce compressor/chiller intensity per MW** in some climates. Avoid simplistic growth claims that all chillers grow equally.
- **Vertiv** CoolChip CDU 2300 + thermal-chain, 2.3 MW NVIDIA DSX ready, controls and power UPS; nearest complete high-power turnkey infrastructure.
- **Modine Airedale** CDU 400kW–2MW+, chillers, dry coolers and rack systems; after PT divest it is more focused but customer concentration and supply chain inventory risk high.
- **nVent** direct liquid CDUs, manifolds, RDHx, integrated racks, AMD Helios.
- **Market size, definitions must match**: Dell'Oro Aug 19 2026 forecasts *all data center physical infrastructure* equipment revenue **22% CAGR 2025–2030 to $120B**; thermal management fastest DCPI segment. Dell'Oro Jan 8 2026 *liquid cooling equipment* manufacturer revenues **~$7B by 2029**. GMI Sep 2026 broader *data center cooling equipment* forecast 2026 $24.2B→2035 $68.7B (~12.3% CAGR), **not interchangeable with Dell'Oro $7B liquid-only or DCPI $120B**. Different market definitions and forecast assumptions.

## Practical thesis-validation ranking, not buy ranking
- Pure commercial cooling quality & integrated services: **VRT** first deeper financial audit. Growth organic +55% but valuation expectations/AI capex cyclical risk high. Verify order quality vs cancellations, services gross margin, normalized 2027–29 sales, ROIC.
- High growth & event risk: **MOD** first for growth *and* biggest near-term diligence on post-spin GAAP/FCF and Samsung M&A rumor. Q2 DC gross margin down 470bp; FY2027 guidance 80–90% DC growth and $4B multiyear capacity LTA; determine minimum take-or-pay versus optional capacity.
- Lower multiple/diversified electrical-cooling mix: **NVT** first balanced risk/return comparison; not all group sales cooling, quantify earnings contribution from liquid cooling.
- Orbital option technology (not ground hedge): **RDW** smallsat Q-Rad only; no space AI radiator sales. Track NASA thermal technology commercialization and issuer share issuance.
- Components/integration: **PH**, **FLEX** secondary, but PH datacenter only ~1.5% FY2026 sales, FLEX CPI likely separate stock 2027. No claim dual orbital+ground monetization.
- International competitor screen: China **Envicool 002837.SZ**, Taiwan **Auras 3324.TWO** and others by device class, ongoing competition on liquid loops and CDUs. Chinese supply chain/outside US exposure, trading and accounting access constraints.

## Follow-up due diligence gates
1. **Valuation matched-period**: GAAP P/E, pro forma EV/EBIT, adjusted vs GAAP FCF, EV/FCF, order backlog quality, organic annual growth excluding FX/M&A, ROI and dilution; normalize Modine spin-off and potential takeover.
2. **Cooling-specific sales** (vs consolidated growth): VRT Thermal segment revenue not separately reported standard, NVT liquid cooling mix not isolated, Modine DC independently reported. Avoid company +55% = cooling +55%.
3. **Technology architecture vs vendor moat**: NVIDIA DSX / AMD rack qualification, CDU liquid performance heat transfer kW per dollar, service/SLA, coolant purity/leaks, supply reliability, higher-temp dry-cooled impact, hyperscaler in-sourcing and Chinese competitors (Google–Envicool discussions, not contracts).
4. **Space exit conditions**: first verifiable 100kW+ in orbit thermal disposal, W/kg and m²/kW optimized radiator verified in thermal vacuum + orbit, 3–5yr risk, signed contracts with actual satellites, pricing against terrestrial computing. Do not value orbital TAM as near-term bookings.
