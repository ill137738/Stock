## Korea tax advantage, confirmed official 2026
Assuming **Korean tax-resident individual holding directly listed stocks, below large shareholder threshold, sale through exchange**:
- Korea listed stock capital gain **non-taxable for ordinary small shareholder**; large shareholder definition KOSPI stake >=1% OR holdings >=₩5B at prior FY end, with special related party aggregation rules; OTC sales otherwise taxable. 2026 KOSPI disposal securities transaction tax + rural special tax **0.05%+0.15%=0.20% of gross SALE proceeds**, even at loss. Dividends generally 15.4% WHT subject to financial-income comprehensive inclusion if aggregate interest+dividends >₩20M/year and special dividend policies if any.
- Foreign listed stocks capital gain **20% income tax + 2% local =22%** after one **annual ₩2.5M tax base exemption combined for taxable domestic and foreign stocks**; regular Korea exchange small-share gain tax-exempt so losses on such holdings cannot offset taxable foreign gains. Overseas sale not subject Korea KOSPI 0.2% transfer levy but foreign local transfer/SDRT, brokerage etc may apply. Foreign dividends withholding depends on tax treaty and Korean foreign tax credit/offset; do NOT assume every foreign dividend simply 15.4%.
- **Illustration:** ₩100M buys one Korean KOSPI or foreign stock, both sold for ₩150M after duration; **Korea** gains 50M - gross proceeds ×0.2% 0.3M = after sale gain ₩49.7M, assuming 0 capital gains tax. **foreign** capital gains 50M less sole annual 2.5M exemption →47.5M×22%=₩10.45M, after-tax gain ₩39.55M, ignoring local market transfer taxes, fees, FX and dividends; advantage ₩10.15M or **25.66% of foreign after-tax profit**. Example only and not actual account tax outcome.
- For higher gain/long horizon, Korea small-share tax edge economically important, hence Hanwha can rank more highly **for Korean resident after-tax expected-return** than global pre-tax ranking. But superior pre-tax global firm could still win if it generates > required extra after-tax return; Korean won concentration and Korea-specific equity governance can harm returns. Evaluate currency risk, interest, liquidity and tax changes over holding.
