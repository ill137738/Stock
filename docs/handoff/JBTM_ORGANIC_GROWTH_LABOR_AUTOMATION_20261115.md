# JBTM organic vs reported growth, FY2026 revenue forecast, labor automation and pandemic case

**Research date:** 2026-11-15 KST. **Status:** Research only; JBTM/PAHC core unchanged, NO_BUY_APPROVAL. USD unless stated.

## A. Owner's calculation question: 4.3% through September ≠ 6% in December
- **2026 Jan–Sep net revenue:** $2,911M vs 2025 Jan–Sep $2,790M, reported growth +$121M / $2,790M = **+4.34%**, rounded 4.3%. The percentage already compares **equivalent nine-month periods**, so do **not** extrapolate by multiplying 12/9 or adding 1.7 points to get a 6% full-year outcome.
- Official organic / FX bridge: **$51M / prior nine-month base = +1.8pp organic** (same-business real pricing/volume/mix, excluding FX) plus **$70M = +2.5pp favorable currency translation**. In the 2026 nine-month bridge, no separate positive acquisition effect is identified, but organic terminology generally controls for M&A/currency; not literal physical volume growth.
- **FY2025 revenue $3,798.2M** [10-K]. **2026 FY November 3 guidance $3,950–$4,000M** [Q3 release], implies reported **+4.0% to +5.3% YoY**; midpoint $3,975M +4.65% (company rounds to ~5%), includes expected **~1.5pp FX benefit**. FY organic projection is broadly ~3% range if other bridge components small; this is **an inference, not independently issued organic-guidance figure**.
- 2026 Q4 revenue needed at FY guide: $3,950−$2,911=$1,039M to $4,000−$2,911=$1,089M. FY2025 Q4 revenue ~$3,798.2−$2,790=$1,008.2M, so guide implies Q4 **+3.1% to +8.0%** yoy.
- **Full-year +6%** requires $3,798.2×1.06=$4,026.1M, Q4 $4,026.1−$2,911=$1,115.1M, **~+10.6% Q4 yoy**, above current management upper FY revenue guidance. Plausible only if beat and/or guide raised, not an established result.
- Latest **2026 Q3** company revenues $994M vs $1,001M, **−0.7% YoY**. Protein +6.3%, Prepared/Food/Beverage −6.3%. Company attributed softer Prepared book-and-ship CPG demand and reduced FY revenue guide despite strong gross/adj EBITDA margin realization. Historical 1.3% conservative growth was hypothetical 2026–2035, not actual 2026.

## B. Why automation and fewer workers can benefit business
- Food factory client ROI includes (i) reduced needed workers per kg, (ii) throughput with shift fewer people, (iii) consistent deboning/fish fillet yield, (iv) less trim/rework and contamination/defective product, (v) complex SKU changes and ready-meals quality, (vi) remote monitoring/aftermarket lifecycle. Labor expense is not the only benefit in low-wage emerging markets.
- **2026 JBT Marel Investor Day** identifies labor scarcity and skilled-worker shortage, more need for yield/throughput, integrated high-speed lines and digital machine vision. **ATHENA** presented customer case with >60% reduction of labor in **three specific steps** (loading/harvesting/trimming), *not an entire plant or portfolio median*. Scale depends on installation rate, payback, service uptime and scarce maintenance skills.
- Factory automation grows more during sustained labor scarcity/wage increases than as direct immediate benefit from every acute pandemic; disruptions can shut customer plants and delay CAPEX and site access. New equipment requires on-site work, financing and some skilled operators.
- Hypothetical simple labor-only example (NOT a company claim): factory replaces 10 full-time equivalent roles costing $45,000 each loaded = $450k annual savings; assuming an integrated automation project costs $2M plus $100k incremental annual maintenance, labor-only net saving $350k/year → **~5.7-year simple payback** before yield, uptime, capex interest, taxes. Adding validated yield savings can shorten payback, but overpromising equipment productivity undermines credibility. Low-wage facilities with same CAPEX may have a much longer labor-only payback.

## C. Actual pandemic outcomes — two-sided evidence
- **2020 JBT FoodTech** revenue $1,234.5M vs FY2019 $1,329.4M → **−7.14%** reported. 2020 JBT note: 1H organic company revenue fell 9% because customers suspended/deferring equipment investments; customer plant visits, aftermarket and JBT installation hampered. This is direct refutation of simplistic 'COVID means immediate equipment sales surge'.
- **2021 JBT FoodTech** sales $1,400.4M vs $1,234.5M → **+13.4%** reported. New FoodTech equipment orders **$1,620.1M vs $1,252.7M in 2020 → +29.3%**; rebound had general reopening, QSR/packaged/ready-meals demand, customer capex normalization, and automation drivers; impossible to attribute the entire improvement solely to pandemic-induced automation.
- **2020 standalone Marel annual report:** pandemic heightened workforce shortages and food safety, increased customer interest in robotics and digitalization. Its 2020 Q2 press release notes orders **−10% YoY** even as companies considered automation: crisis caused immediate order deferrals. This was before JBTM acquired Marel in 2025.
- For investment: **near term adverse shock** (closures/customer cash/installation restrictions) vs **medium/long term automation acceleration** (scarce labor, food safety, traceability and resilience). Structural labor shortages may stimulate demand without a pandemic. No data demonstrate JBTM as a positive stock-price hedge during a lockdown.

## D. Evaluation gates
1. Quantify 2027+ regional equipment organic bookings and cancellation/capex sensitivity; split Protein vs Prepared end-market.
2. Track customer ROI reference cases (jobs saved, wage, line CAPEX, annual maintenance, yield improvement) and independent empirical comparisons; don't generalize >60% reduced labor for certain tasks.
3. Distinguish short-term shutdown demand loss from long-term adoption/installed-base aftermarkets, especially FX, revenue guidance and margins.
